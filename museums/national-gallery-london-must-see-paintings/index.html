7 must-see artworks at the National Gallery

Free to enter and right on Trafalgar Square, the National Gallery covers European painting from the 13th to the early 20th century.

Updated: 2026-11-22

The must-see works

1The Arnolfini Portrait

Jan van Eyck · 1434

What to look for: Lean in to the convex mirror: it reflects the whole room and two more figures standing in the doorway.

2The Fighting Temeraire

J. M. W. Turner · 1839

What to look for: A ghostly warship towed to the breakers by a dark steam tug at sunset: the end of the age of sail.

3Sunflowers

Vincent van Gogh · 1888

What to look for: Yellow on yellow. Van Gogh painted it to decorate Gauguin's room in Arles.

4The Hay Wain

John Constable · 1821

What to look for: A cart in the river Stour in Suffolk. Look for the white flecks of paint that make the light sparkle.

5Bathers at Asnières

Georges Seurat · 1884

What to look for: Seurat's first large painting, before he fully developed pointillism. Factories smoke in the background.

6The Ambassadors

Hans Holbein the Younger · 1533

What to look for: Stand close to the right side and look back: the strange shape at the bottom turns into a skull.

7Whistlejacket

George Stubbs · c. 1762

What to look for: A life-size racehorse rearing against a plain background, with no rider and no landscape.

Tips for your visit

  • Entry to the permanent collection is free; booking a free slot is recommended at busy times.
  • View The Ambassadors from the right side to see the hidden skull.
  • Pick up a floor plan: the gallery is a maze of rooms.
